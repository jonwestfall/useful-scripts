The Podium Guide · version 1.1

Teach from anywhere in the room.

Podium turns the classroom computer into a screen you drive from your iPad or phone. Open one browser tab on the machine wired to the projector and walk away. Slides, PDFs, video, handwriting, timers, polls and your phone's camera all come from the device in your hand.

JW

Made in a classroom, by a teacher

Podium isn't a company or a product team. It's one professor's answer to a problem every teacher knows: the lecture is happening out in the room, and the computer running the projector is stuck behind the lectern.

Every feature here exists because a real class needed it: a video cued up without the room watching me hunt for it, a timer for group work, a poll that doesn't need an app, captions for a student who needs them. It stays deliberately simple: a few web pages you can read, host for free, and trust.

Jon Westfall · Professor, with more than twenty years in the classroom · designer and maintainer of Podium

How it works

A few web pages, each with one job

Podium is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript that any modern browser runs directly. The devices never talk to each other directly. They all check in with a small relay, and everything they say is sealed with your room's passphrase before it leaves the browser.

Swipe the diagram sideways to see all of it.

The display

display.html

Runs fullscreen on the classroom PC. No toolbars, no cursor, no notifications. It shows what it's told and remembers the lecture through a reload.

The controller

control.html

Your remote on an iPad, iPhone or laptop. It holds the library, presenter notes, ink, timers, polls, music and camera. Several controllers can share one room.

The planning desk

plan.html

For your office. Build a lecture's running order, then carry it to class as a single file, or send it straight to your own server.

The audience

join.html · view.html

Students answer polls on their own phones with no app and no login. Guest viewers watch the screen on their own device and can't change anything.

A class period with Podium

From your office to the end of class

Here's how the pieces fit into an ordinary teaching day.

  1. The night before

    Plan it at your desk

    • Drag decks, PDFs, clips and notes into a running order.
    • Set a target length and saved countdowns.
    • Choose what auto-launches when you open it.

  2. Ten minutes before

    Set the room

    • Press G on the classroom PC to go live.
    • Scan the pairing QR with your iPad.
    • Start background music and a "We begin in…" countdown.

  3. During class

    Teach, don't click

    • Freeze, line up the next thing, then TAKE it.
    • Write on slides with ink and point with the laser.
    • Run a poll, show a document camera, put up captions.

  4. After class

    Keep what mattered

    • Export annotated slides and boards as a ZIP or PDF.
    • Download poll results as CSV.
    • On your own server, get a lecture recap PDF.

Presenting

Show anything, and never cut to something you haven't looked at first.

Details →
🎬

Freeze is a cue

Borrowed from a TV control room. Freeze holds the projector still while you flip ahead, scrub a video past its ad, or open page 42 of a PDF. TAKE cuts it live instantly, Swap trades live and cued, and Blank blacks out the room with everything still loaded underneath. Turn on Always cue first and every pick waits in the cue.

🔭

Now, Next & Notes

A confidence monitor in your hand: what the room sees, what's coming (down to the next bullet of a build), and your presenter notes. Jump to any slide from a searchable thumbnail grid.

📝

Slides in Markdown

Marp decks rendered live in the browser, with KaTeX math, progressive builds, presenter notes and your department's own CSS theme.

📄

PDFs & PowerPoint

Page through PDFs, and zoom and pan the projector into a figure. On your own server, .pptx uploads become PDFs automatically.Server

🗂️

A library of anything

  • Decks, picture decks, PDFs, HTML slides
  • Video, audio and YouTube
  • Photos, web pages, text signs, QR codes
  • Whiteboard, chalkboard, black, timers

Paste any link, or upload a photo from the iPad.

🪟

Split screen & picture-in-picture

Single, side-by-side, stacked, one-big-two-small, a quad grid, or a PiP inset in any corner. Each panel keeps its own item, zoom and ink.

↩️

Back to where you were

Wander off to a photo mid-deck and the Back to strip returns you to the exact slide you left, not to the start of the deck.

Drawing & pointing

Write on anything with an Apple Pencil, a stylus or a finger.

Details →
✏️

Live ink

Pen, highlighter and a stroke-by-stroke eraser in any colour and thickness. Ink stays pinned to the slide, and each slide keeps its own drawing.

📐

Clean shapes

Pause at the end of a stroke and it snaps into a straight line, arrow, rectangle or circle. Undo and Undo clear rescue a slip.

🔴

Laser pointer

Drag across the Now box and a red, green or blue dot follows on the projector, gone the moment you lift your finger.

🔆

Spotlight

Dim everything except a circle around your finger, to point the whole room at one line of an equation.

Media & sound

A real transport and a real mixer, operated from across the room.

Video, audio & YouTube

Play, pause, scrub, skip ±10s and loop, without the YouTube site or its recommendations ever reaching the projector.

Background music

Playlists for while the room fills. It ducks under lecture video, fades out over three seconds, and can put a "We begin in…" countdown on screen.

🎚️

Separate faders for content, music and microphones, a Master on every tab, and one-tap mute.

Your iPad as a microphone

Amplify a controller's mic through the room's speakers. On your own server you can also record it with the lecture.Server

On-screen tools

The small things a lecture needs, one tap away.

Details →
⏱️

Countdown timers

Up to four at once, run by the display so they stay accurate if your iPad sleeps. Split the screen to show two side by side.

💬

Full-screen messages

"Back in 5", a discussion prompt, a title card. Light Markdown, five fonts, colours and an optional picture, with a live preview.

🏷️

Watermarks

Your name, course number or logo pinned to a corner through every change. Courses on your own server can set a default.

🔁

Automated sets

A rotation that runs itself (announcements, a QR code, lab rules), each item held for its own seconds, in order or shuffled.

Engaging the room

Participation with no apps, no accounts, and no third-party service.

Details →
🗳️

Audience pollsServer

  • Multiple choice, with an optional correct answer for quizzes
  • Short answer, with a word cloud
  • Q&A where students upvote questions
  • Voting timers, optional names, CSV export

Results stay on your iPad until you tap Reveal to room.

📷

Document camera

Your phone streams its camera to the projector over a direct connection. Freeze frames of student work and hold four up side by side, each ready for ink.

📺

Guest View

Anyone can watch the live screen, with sound, on their own phone or laptop: the back row, or someone not in the room. It's watch-only and signed by the display, and presenter notes and the cue are never included.

Accessibility

For the students in the room, and for the person at the front of it.

Audit →
🗣️

Live captions

Your speech, captioned along the bottom of the projector by the browser's own speech recognition. Off by default, with its privacy trade-off stated plainly.

📜

Pre-scripted captions

Write a caption or audio description for any planned item. It appears the moment that item goes live, and never overwrites a live transcript.

♿

Screen readers & contrast

🌗

Light & high contrast

A high-contrast light theme for bright rooms and window glare, or follow the system setting.

Sharing the lectern

Co-teachers, TAs and substitutes, without handing over your iPad.

Details →
🤝

Pair in seconds

The display shows a QR code. Scan it and the controller opens already configured, then the code hides itself after 90 seconds. Matching four-character codes confirm both ends agree.

👥

Many controllers, one room

An instructor's iPad, a TA's phone and a laptop at the back all stay in sync, each showing the same cue and the same screen.

🧑‍🏫

Simple Mode for substitutes

A guest link gives a big-button clicker: Next, Previous, Play, Blank and a laser pad. It can't disturb anything you've frozen or cued.

Privacy & reliability

Built for campus Wi-Fi and a live audience

Classroom networks are shared and a lecture can't be restarted, so Podium assumes the network is watched and that it might drop.

🔒 Sealed in the browser

AES-256

Every message is encrypted (AES-GCM) before it leaves the device. The relay routes by room name and sees only ciphertext.

150,000

PBKDF2 rounds turn your passphrase into the key, which makes guessing slow. Use three or four random words.

#

The pairing link keeps the passphrase after the #, the part of a URL browsers never send to a server.

0

No analytics, no tracking, no company holding your lecture material.

🛟 Keeps going when things go wrong

Offline

A service worker caches the pages, so loaded slides, ink and timers keep working if the campus network drops.

Reload

The display resumes the lecture after a crash or refresh, or starts black if you prefer.

Build

Out-of-date copies are detected. A controller says which device is running old code and offers to reload it.

Tests

Over two dozen unit suites and a browser end-to-end suite run on every change, covering freeze safety, ink, audio math and encryption.

Stated plainly: two things step outside the encryption, and both are opt-in. Audience polls go through your own server, because students' phones don't hold the room key. Live captions in Chrome and Edge send audio to Google for recognition, while Safari recognizes speech on the device. The architecture guide explains both.

Keyboard shortcuts

For a clicker, a keyboard, or the lectern PC

On the controller

  • B FBlank or freeze the screen
  • T CTAKE the cue live, or clear it
  • ← → SpacePage through a deck, or play and pause media
  • P ⇧PPhotograph the focused panel, or the whole screen
  • W M RWhiteboard, music, run the selected timer

On the classroom display

  • GGo live: fullscreen, sound, and keep the screen awake
  • PShow or hide the pairing QR code
  • FToggle fullscreen
  • EEnd of class: back to the Go live screen
  • SOpen Settings
  • BBack to the Podium home page
  • EscClose whatever is open
